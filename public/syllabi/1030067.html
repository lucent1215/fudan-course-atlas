课程简介（英文）

Objectives:

This course teaches students the fundamental principles and methods of modern macroe- conomic theory, and to illustrate the usefulness of these principles and methods for studying economic problems in practice. The objective is to help the students develop their own ways of thinking about real life economic issues. For that, both economic intuitions and scientific treatments will be emphasized.

Prerequisites:

Students are expected to have completed an introductory course in calculus. Although the basic tools of mathematical optimization will be reviewed in class, students are expected to be comfortable with taking partial derivatives and solving a system of first order conditions.

Lecture Notes and Reference Books:

Part of Professor Wang’s lectures will be based on the notes that will be sent to the students through email as classes proceed. At the same time, students are strongly advised to take notes in class, as the written lecture notes would not include all materials covered. This course does not have a required textbook, but students are recommended to purchase the following as a reference book from which readings may be assigned. This reference book also covers most of the economic models that we will introduce and study in the course.

• Macroeconomics; author: Gregory Mankiw; publisher: Worth. Any edition of the book will be good for our purpose.

Grading:

Final grades will be determined by the student’s performance on six to eight problem sets (20% of the course grade), one midterm exam (30%), and a comprehensive final exam (50%). The lowest of the scores on the problem sets will be omitted when computing the student’s overall grade. The midterm exam will take place some time towards the end of April during regular class time. The midterm exam is 90-100 minutes long. The final exam will take place during the final examination period. Makeup exams will be given only in the event of (documented) family or medical emergencies.

Problem Sets:

Completed problem sets can be handed in only to the TA on the due date after class. Problem sets are due usually one week after they are assigned. Problem sets that are turned in late will receive a grade of zero without exception. Some of the problem set questions are designed to be more challenging, students may work together when solving these problem sets, but final answers must be written in their own words. Copying of another student’s work will not receive credit. In addition, answers to the problem set questions should be neat and clean, and be written in good English. Sloppy and/or obscure answers will not receive full credit.

教学目标

This course teaches students the fundamental principles and methods of modern macroeconomic theory, and to illustrate the usefulness of these principles and methods for studying economic problems in practice. The objective is to help the students develop their own ways ofunderstanding and  thinking about real life macroeconomic issues. For that, both economic intuitions and scientific treatments will be emphasized.

教学方式

The course will be taught by Professor Wang in 15 lectures which cover the classical IS-LM model for macroeconomic analysis, and the more contemporary macroeconomic theories of economic growth, and models of the labor and capital markets. An emphasis will be placed on teaching the students the methods of developing and using macroeconomic models, for understanding economic data and policy.

Students are expected to attend all the lectures, write the midterm and final exams, and submit their answers to all the problem sets.
