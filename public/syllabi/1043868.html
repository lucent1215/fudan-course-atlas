课程简介（中文）

本课程通过七个主题单元探讨医学、伦理学与人文学科的交叉领域。每个单元为期两周，采用课堂讲授、多媒体资源、案例研究和协作项目相结合的教学方式。学生将深入探讨生命伦理学、叙事医学和全球健康等主题，同时培养批判性思维、沟通能力和伦理决策技能。课程着重强调反思实践、跨学科学习以及基于团队合作的问题解决能力。

课程简介（英文）

This course explores the intersection of medicine, ethics, and humanities through seven thematic units. Each unit spans 2 weeks, combining lectures, multimedia resources, case studies, and collaborative projects. Students will engage with topics such as bioethics, narrative medicine, and global health, while developing critical thinking, communication, and ethical decision-making skills. The course emphasizes reflective practice, interdisciplinary learning, and team-based problem-solving.

教学目标

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

  • analyze ethical dilemmas in medical practice (e.g., informed consent, clinical trials) and reflect on historical scandals like the Tuskegee Syphilis Experiment.
  • critically evaluate the sociocultural meanings of illness, including stigma and discrimination associated with AIDS and cancer.
  • compare models of patient-professional relationships (paternalistic vs. patient-centered) and demonstrate effective communicative skills.
  • apply narrative medicine techniques to understand patient perspectives and reflect on the meaning of doctoring.
  • integrate liberal arts (literature, music, visual arts) into medical practice to enhance empathy and holistic care.
  • debate ethical implications of medical advancements (genomics, AI) and their societal impacts.
  • advocate for patient-centered approaches in end-of-life care, balancing quality of life versus longevity.
  • collaborate on solutions for global health challenges, including pandemics and cross-border health equity.

教学方式

  • Flipped Classroom: Pre-class exploration of concepts (e.g., “Medical Scandals”) followed by in-depth discussions.
  • Multimedia Integration: Video clips, documentaries, and creative writing exercises.
  • Case-Based Learning: Role-play informed consent scenarios, debate CRISPR ethics, or simulate patient narratives.
  • Collaborative Projects: Teams design a “Narrative Medicine Portfolio” or a global health advocacy campaign.
  • Reflective Practice: Journals linking personal experiences to course themes (e.g., “A Time I Witnessed Stigma”).
