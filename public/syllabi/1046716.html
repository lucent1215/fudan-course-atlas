课程简介（中文）

本课程将结合西方文明史发展的不同阶段，选取每一时期具有代表性的叙事文学作品，尝试通过对经典叙事作品的总体介绍和特定文本的微观解读挖掘西方叙事文学发展的主线和重要主题关注，由此体现西方叙事文学乃至文明史发展不同时期的时代特色。

  • 在宏观层面上，本课程将梳理西方叙事体裁历史演变及其文化背景，主要着眼于艺术和现实之间的复杂关系 （mimesis），以及叙事作品（情节模式、主人公、主题）总体模式的整体演变趋势以及这种演变所涉及的社会历史和文化背景。
  • 在微观层面上，将涉及相关叙事基本概念和基本范畴（如故事与话语；叙述声音），帮助学生实现对故事情节的宏观把控的分析手法，介绍母题、情节有机体等相关概念，帮助学生掌握叙述手法和策略的微观分析手法，并将之运用于具体文本解读，培养其对于语言文字的敏感度和整体审美能力，同时也采用跨媒介手段，考察叙事作品在当代所演变出的多种形式可能和相关主题关注。

 

课程简介（英文）

Course Summary: This course traces the developmental stages of Western civilization and selects representative narrative literary works from each historical period. Through general introductions to canonical narratives and close microscopic readings of specific texts, it explores the main developmental threads and core thematic concerns of Western narrative literature, and further illustrates the distinctive cultural features of different historical phases in the evolution of Western narrative art and civilization.
 

教学目标

Teaching Objectives

Macro-level Objectives: This course systematizes the historical evolution of Western narrative genres and their corresponding cultural contexts. It focuses on the complex interplay between art and reality centered on the concept of mimesis, as well as the overall evolutionary patterns of narrative works in terms of plot structures, protagonist archetypes, and dominant themes. It also contextualizes such literary transformations within broader social, historical, and cultural shifts.

Micro-level Objectives: This course introduces fundamental narrative concepts and categories, including story and discourse, narrative voice, motifs, and organic plot structures. It equips students with analytical tools for both macro-level textual comprehension and micro-level stylistic interpretation, cultivating their linguistic sensitivity and comprehensive aesthetic competence. Adopting a cross-media perspective, the course also examines the diversified contemporary forms of narrative practice and their evolving thematic concerns.

Course Schedule & Learning Arrangements

Introduction (Week 1)

Core Teaching Focus

Starting from students’ personal reading experiences, this session briefly sorts out major literary genres and their formal characteristics, and clarifies the core keywords and main logical framework of the course.

Key Reflection Questions

  • What is narrative? What are the essential core elements of narrative?
  • What are the major literary genres involved in the Western narrative tradition?

Part One: Classical Epic and the Tradition of Heroism (Week 2–6)

Required Reading (Self-study after class)

Iliad, Book 1, Book 9, Book 18, Book 24

Weekly Teaching Topics

  • Week 2: General Introduction: The World of Pantheism and The Classical Age
  • Week 3: The Epic Plot: Main Thread and Digressions
  • Week 4: Epic Characters: Achilles and His Inevitable Fate
  • Week 5: The World in Epic: Human Conditions Depicted on the Shield of Achilles — “A Wonder to Behold”
  • Week 6: Priam at the Feet of Achilles: Wrath, Sympathy and Human Hubris

Part Two: From Classic to Modern: Entering the World of Robinson Crusoe (Week 7–10)

Required Reading

Robinson Crusoe (1819, abridged version);Foe (1986) by J. M. Coetzee

Key Reflection Questions

  • What kind of story world does Foe construct? How does it differ from the desert island world inRobinson Crusoe?
  • What are the similarities and differences in narrative techniques and writing styles between the two English novels with a 200-year interval?
  • How are the fundamental differences between realism and modernism embodied in the structural and textual mechanisms of novels?

Weekly Teaching Topics

  • Week 7: Entering the World of Robinson Crusoe
  • Week 8: A Woman’s Narrative: Re-entering Crusoe’s Barren Island
  • Week 9: The Mirror and the Lamp: From Realism to Modernism
  • Week 10: A Backward Glance on the Two Narrative Worlds

Part Three: New Forms and Developments of Western Narrative Genres (Week 11–15)

Required Reading

The Problem of Evil (2003); Maus (1989, 1992) by Art Spiegelman

Key Reflection Questions

  • The media characteristics of graphic novels: the mimetic, constructive and thematic attributes of visual narrative.
  • How to interpret the rise and prosperity of graphic narrative novels since the second half of the 20th century?
  • What other new narrative media can be found in contemporary mass media?

Weekly Teaching Topics

  • Week 11: Historical Memory and Graphic Imagination
  • Week 12: The Bloodstained History of a Murderer
  • Week 13: Memory and Artistic Imagination of Auschwitz
  • Week 14: Why Do We Study Classics in the Contemporary Era?
  • Week 15: New Media and Narrativity: Images and Words in History and Fiction

Course Conclusion (Week 16)

Comprehensive review of course core content, systematic sorting of Western narrative development context, centralized Q&A and academic discussion.
