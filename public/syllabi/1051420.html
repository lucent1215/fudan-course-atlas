课程简介（英文）

Objectives

The aim of this course is to teach students the fundamental principles and methods of modern microeconomic theory, and to illustrate the usefulness of these principles and methods for studying economic problems in practice. The objective is to help the students develop their own ways of thinking about real life economic issues. For that, both economic intuitions and scientific treatments will be emphasized.

Prerequisites

Students are expected to have completed an introductory course in calculus. Although the basic tools of mathematical optimization will be reviewed in class, students are expected to be comfortable with taking partial derivatives and solving a system of first order conditions.

Lecture Notes and Reference Books

Part of Professor Wang's lectures will be based partially on the notes that will be sent to the students through email as classes proceed.

Microeconomics Lecture Notes: Cheng Wang, School of Economics, Fudan University, 2025.

At the same time, students are strongly advised to take notes in class, as the written lecture notes would not include all materials covered. This course does not have a required textbook, but students are recommended to purchase the following as a reference book from which readings may be assigned. This reference book also covers most of the economic models that we will introduce and study in the course.

Intermediate Microeconomics: A Modern Approach, 7th Edition, author: Hal R. Varian, publisher: W.W. Norton & Company (2006), or the latest version (which version is not important).

Grading

Final grades will be determined by the student's performance on six to eight problem sets (20% of the course grade), one midterm exam (30%), and a comprehensive final exam (50%). The lowest of the scores on the problem sets will be omitted when computing the student's overall grade. The midterm exam will take place some time towards the end of April during regular class time. The midterm exam is 90 minutes long. The final exam will take place during the final examination period. Makeup examinations will be given only in the event of (documented) family or medical emergencies.

Completed problem sets can be handed in only to the TA on the due date after class. Problem sets are due usually one week after they are assigned. Problem sets that are turned in late will receive a grade of zero without exception. Some of the problem set questions are designed to be more challenging, students may work together when solving these problem sets, but final answers must be written in their own words. Copying of another student's work will not receive credit. In addition, answers to the  problem set questions should be neat and clear, and be written in good English or Chinese. Sloppy and/or obscure answers will not receive full credit.

教学目标

The aim of this course is to teach students the fundamental principles and methods of modern microeconomic theory, and to illustrate the usefulness of these principles and methods for studying economic problems in practice. The objective is to help the students develop their own ways of thinking about real life economic issues. For that, both economic intuitions and scientific treatments will be emphasized.

教学方式

The course will be taught by Professor Wang in a series of lectures which cover the fundamental principles and methods of modern microeconomic theory, including models of consumers and firms, markets and equilibrium, risk and insurance, private information and contracting, and game theory with applications, as well as an introduction to the economics of AI. An emphasis will be placed on teaching students the methods of developing and using microeconomic models, combining economic intuition with scientific treatment, for understanding real-life economic issues both in theory and in practice.

Students are expected to attend all the lectures, write the midterm and final exams, and submit their answers to all the problem sets.

 

教学内容与进度安排

The course is divided into five parts. In the first part of the course we will discuss models of consumers, firms, markets, and equilibrium, and the second part is devoted to risk and insurance. The third part treats private information and contracting, and the fourth part covers game theory and its applications. A brief discussion of the economics of AI will be given in the fifth part towards the end of the course.

The course will cover a subset but not all of the following topics, depending on how the lectures will develop.
