课程简介（中文）

《计算与智能概念A》是一门面向本科低年级学生开设的计算机与人工智能专业基础导论课程，主要服务于计算与智能创新学院英文项目学生，尤其是具有不同学科背景的国际学生。本课程可作为人工智能全英文本科项目大一上学期的专业入门课程，也可作为其他英文项目学生补充计算机与人工智能基础知识的通识性专业课程。

本课程围绕“计算如何发生、系统如何运行、智能如何实现”这一主线，系统介绍计算机科学与人工智能的核心概念、基本原理和典型应用。课程内容涵盖计算思维、数据表示、算法基础、程序运行机制、计算机系统组成、操作系统与网络基础、软件与硬件协同工作原理，以及人工智能、机器学习、深度学习、大模型和智能系统的基本概念。通过概念讲解、案例分析和轻量级实践训练，学生将逐步建立对计算机系统与人工智能技术的整体认知框架。

通过本课程学习，学生能够理解计算机从硬件到软件、从数据到智能的基本工作流程，认识现代人工智能技术与计算系统之间的关系，掌握后续学习程序设计、数据结构、机器学习、计算机系统、人工智能等课程所需的基础概念和表达方式。同时，课程将帮助学生形成基本的计算思维和问题分析能力，具备开展简单编程实验、数据处理和智能应用体验的初步实践能力，为进入人工智能及相关专业课程学习扫清基础障碍。

课程简介（英文）

Fundamentals of Computing and Intelligence A is an introductory foundational course in school of CS and AI for lower-year undergraduate students. It is designed primarily for English-taught programs in our school and other schools, especially for international students with diverse academic backgrounds. The course can serve as a first-semester introductory course for the English-taught undergraduate program in Artificial Intelligence, and may also be offered as a foundational elective for students from other English-taught programs who wish to develop essential knowledge of computing and AI.

Organized around the central questions of “how computation takes place, how computer systems operate, and how intelligence is realized,” this course introduces the core concepts, fundamental principles, and representative applications of computer science and artificial intelligence. Topics include computational thinking, data representation, basic algorithms, program execution mechanisms, computer system organization, fundamentals of operating systems and networks, software-hardware collaboration, as well as key concepts in artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning, large language models, and intelligent systems. Through conceptual lectures, case studies, and lightweight hands-on practice, students will gradually build a comprehensive conceptual framework for understanding computing systems and AI technologies.

Upon completing the course, students will understand the basic workflow of computers from hardware to software and from data to intelligence; recognize the relationship between modern AI technologies and computing systems; and master the foundational concepts and terminology needed for subsequent courses such as programming, data structures, machine learning, computer systems, and artificial intelligence. The course will also help students develop basic computational thinking and problem-analysis skills, together with preliminary practical abilities in simple programming experiments, data processing, and intelligent application exploration.

教学目标

This course aims to help students build a foundational and systematic understanding of computing and artificial intelligence. Through the course, students will understand how computers represent data, execute programs, organize hardware and software resources, and support intelligent applications. Students will become familiar with key concepts in computational thinking, algorithms, computer systems, networks, artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning, and large language models. The course also aims to develop students’ ability to analyze computing-related problems, use basic technical terminology correctly, and complete simple hands-on tasks involving programming, data processing, and AI application exploration. By the end of the course, students will be prepared for subsequent study in programming, data structures, computer systems, machine learning, and artificial intelligence, with a clearer understanding of the knowledge structure and learning pathway of the discipline.

教学方式

This course adopts a combination of lectures, case-based learning, guided discussions, and hands-on practice. Lectures will introduce fundamental concepts and knowledge frameworks in computing systems and artificial intelligence. Real-world cases will be used to help students understand how computing principles and AI technologies are applied in practical scenarios. Guided discussions will encourage students to compare concepts, ask questions, and connect computing knowledge with their own disciplinary backgrounds. Lightweight lab activities and demonstrations will be arranged to help students gain preliminary experience in programming, data processing, system observation, and AI application exploration. The course emphasizes conceptual understanding, active participation, and step-by-step skill development.

Key Contents

1

Introduction: From Computing to Intelligence

Introduce the course objectives, learning pathway, and the relationship between computer science and artificial intelligence; understand the basic framework of “data–computation–systems–intelligence.”

2

Fundamentals of Artificial Intelligence

Introduce the development history, basic tasks, typical application scenarios, and fundamental components of intelligent systems in artificial intelligence.

3

Introduction to Machine Learning and Deep Learning

Introduce the basic concepts of supervised learning, unsupervised learning, neural networks, training, and inference.

4

Large Models and Intelligent Applications

Introduce large language models, generative artificial intelligence, prompts, agents, and typical AI applications, helping students develop an intuitive understanding of modern AI systems.

5

Data Representation and Basic Computation

Introduce binary representation, the representation of numbers and text, the representation of images and multimedia data, and how computers process data.

6

Basics of Chips and Computer Organization

Introduce the basic components of a computer, such as the CPU, GPU, memory, buses, and input/output devices, and understand how computer hardware works together to complete computational tasks.

7

 Introduction to Computer Architecture

Introduce basic concepts such as instructions, pipelines, parallel computing, and memory hierarchy, and understand how hardware architecture affects program performance.

8

Computing Hardware for AI

Introduce the basic ideas behind AI computing hardware such as GPUs, TPUs, and NPUs, and understand the relationship between matrix computation, parallel computing, and the execution of AI models.

9

Fundamentals of Operating Systems

Introduce basic concepts such as processes, threads, memory management, file systems, and device management, and understand how operating systems manage hardware and software resources.

10

Basics of Networks and Distributed Systems

Introduce the basic concepts of computer networks, the Internet, the client/server model, cloud computing, and distributed computing.

11

AI Systems and AI OS Concepts

Introduce traditional operating systems and system support for AI-oriented applications, including introductory knowledge of model deployment, resource scheduling, inference services, and operating environments for intelligent devices.

12

 Programming and Program Execution

Introduce high-level languages, the program execution process, interpreted and compiled execution, and the relationship between programs and hardware.

13

Fundamentals of Compilation

Introduce the basic role of compilers and fundamental concepts such as lexical analysis, syntax analysis, intermediate representation, optimization, and code generation.

14

Introduction to AI Compilation

Introduce the basic process by which AI models move from training frameworks to hardware execution, and understand the roles of computational graphs, operators, tensor computation, and compiler optimization.

15

 Examples of AI Programming and Computing Frameworks

Introduce, at an introductory level, the basic ideas behind tools such as CUDA C, Triton, and TileLang, and understand how they help developers express and optimize AI computing tasks.

16

 Review and Mini Project Presentation

Review the relationships among artificial intelligence, hardware, systems, and compilation; students present simple practical tasks or course learning summaries.

 

Module 1: Fundamentals of Artificial Intelligence
Weeks 1–4. This module helps students first build interest and a general understanding from the perspective of AI applications and intelligent systems. It introduces the basic concepts of artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning, and large models.

Module 2: Computer Architecture: Chips and Computer Organization
Weeks 5–8. This module introduces data representation, computer organization, processors, memory systems, and AI computing hardware. It helps students understand the hardware foundations that support intelligent applications.

Module 3: Systems: Traditional Operating Systems and AI OS
Weeks 9–11. This module introduces operating systems, networks, cloud computing, distributed systems, and AI system runtime environments. It helps students understand how software systems manage resources and support AI applications.

Module 4: Compilation and AI Compilation
Weeks 12–15. This module introduces program execution, compiler fundamentals, AI compilation workflows, and beginner-level concepts of AI computing programming tools such as CUDA C, Triton, and TileLang.
