课程简介（中文）

本课程旨在培养学生对于公共政策分析中所需的统计推理与数据分析基础技能。在课程中，学生将学习如何利用数据分析工具理解公共政策问题、评估政策效果，并解释描述单个变量及变量之间关系的关键统计参数。课程将结合公共管理与公共政策研究中的典型案例，例如政府绩效与公共服务供给等议题，帮助学生理解统计分析在政策制定与政策评估中的实际作用。课程的主要分析工具为普通最小二乘法（OLS）回归，随着课程推进，也可能介绍其他统计模型和研究设计。课程的目标是让学生掌握统计技术的应用能力，能够以清晰易懂的语言解释结果，并从数据中做出合理的推断。

课程简介（英文）

This course aims to develop students’ foundational skills in statistical reasoning and data analysis in public policy analysis. Students will learn how to use data analysis tools to understand public policy issues, evaluate policy outcomes, and interpret key statistical parameters that describe individual variables and relationships among variables. The course incorporates typical cases from public policy research to help students understand the practical role of statistical analysis in policymaking and policy evaluation. The primary analytical tool used in the course is Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression, although additional statistical models and research designs may also be introduced as the course progresses. The goal of the course is to equip students with the ability to apply statistical techniques, communicate findings in clear and accessible language, and draw reasonable inferences from data.

教学目标

This course aims to achieve the following objectives:

  • Cultivating a scientific and critical thinking for undergraduate students who are interested in public policy analysis with quantitative methods (“价值引领”);
  • Teaching conceptual and theoretical basics for understanding public policy process and examining policy choices (“知识传授”);
  • Filling the capability gap of students with various disciplinary backgrounds to prepare for a more focused study in policy science (“能力培养”)

Equipping students with practical, state-of-the-art econometric techniques to establish a solid foundation for their future research in quantitative analysis (“技能训练”)

 

教学方式

Lectures as the main component (讲授为主)

The instructor will deliver the course content primarily by lectures. Class discussion and student presentations will be used as the complementary components of the course.

2) Details on Final Essay (Term Paper):

A major component of this course is an individual project designed to give students hands-on experience in applying quantitative methods to real-world policy issues. To support progress, student will submit work at each stage of the project.

 

Step 1: Select a policy topic and identify your research question (5%)

Select a policy issue of interest, such as health, education, transportation, social welfare, or environmental policy, and identify your research question.

PDA Submission 1 (Week 3): A short topic proposal (1–2 pages) that introduces the policy area, explains its relevance, and identify your research question with justifications.

 

Step 2: Gather and manage data (5%)

Collect relevant public datasets. Students are expected to clean, organize, and prepare the data for analysis. Describe how you are going to collect and use data.

PDA Submission 2 (Week 5): A data report (1–2 pages) describing the data source(s), how the data were collected, key variables, and how the dataset will be used for analysis.

 

Step 3: Conduct Quantitative Analysis (15%)
Apply the econometric and statistical methods taught in class to examine your policy issue. This stage should go beyond descriptive statistics and demonstrate your ability to:

  • Usebivariate analysis (contingency tables, correlations, t-tests) to explore relationships between key variables.
  • Buildregression models (typically OLS with multiple variables) to test hypotheses and evaluate policy impacts.
  • Applycausal modelling techniques (intervening variables, indirect effects, antecedent variables) where relevant, to strengthen the robustness of your findings (optional, only if you want to add this in your final essay)

PDA Submission 3 (Week 10): An analysis report (2–5 pages) including descriptive statistics, model specifications, and preliminary results.

 

Step 4: Present findings (15%)

Deliver a 3–6-minute presentation to the class, highlighting the data, methodology, results, and key takeaways. The presentation should clearly communicate what we have learnt in our class, including research question, hypothesis, univariate analysis, bivariate analysis, and so on. Students will present their PDA individually. The instructor will randomly ask several questions based on students’ presentation.

PDA Submission 4 (Week 15): A presentation file (slides or equivalent) highlighting the policy context, data, methods, results, and conclusions.

 

Step 5: Final essay (20%)

Submit an individual written report (final essay) that clearly presents the policy context, data sources, methodology, results, and key takeaways. The essay should highlight how empirical findings contribute to understanding or evaluating the policy issue.

PDA Submission 5 (Week 16): A final essay (typically 8–15 pages) that presents the policy context, data sources, methodology, results, and key conclusions, emphasizing the contribution of empirical findings to policy evaluation.

 

This project emphasizes practical data skills, critical thinking, and evidence-based reasoning. Students are expected to demonstrate the ability to transform raw data into actionable insights that inform understanding of policy outcomes.
