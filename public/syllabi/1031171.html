课程简介（中文）

我们现在生活在一个快速城市化的世界，全球超过一半人口生活在城市。中国是全球城市化最快、城市人口最多的国家，在全球范围内具有重要影响力。大规模快速城市化成为经济和社会发展的强大引擎，也带来一系列公共政策挑战。课程以当代城市社会基础理论与实践挑战为基础，广泛介绍全球城市经验，同时重点探讨中国城市化发展的特色理论与实践经验。

课程简介（英文）

We now live in a rapidly urbanizing world: half of the world’s population now lives in cities and all trends point to the rise of scores of multi-million “megacities” and a dramatic increase in urban populations across Asia, Latin America, and Africa. Cities highlight the best and worst of everything and also reveal the most remarkable contradictions of our time. This course analyzes the public policy challenges faced by urban society. The aim of the course is, first, to provide students with an understanding of global urbanisation and urban problems; second, to prepare students to design, analyze, and evaluate urban public policies. Throughout the course, we will take a comparative approach to the topics, not only drawing case studies drawn from different regions in the world but also paying particular attention to the ongoing urban transformation in China.

教学目标

1. Knowledge
Students will develop a comprehensive understanding of fundamental contemporary urbanisation and development theories. Specifically, they will learn about the processes, characteristics, driving forces, policy outcomes, and challenges associated with China’s urbanisation.

2. Skills
Through conducting a research project, students will become proficient in comparative urban studies methodologies. Students will learn to critically observe, analyse, and interpret urbanisation trends and policies in diverse national and regional contexts.

3. Competencies for lifelong learning

Collaborating on a research project improves students' organisational skills, communication abilities, and capacity to work effectively as part of a team. Additionally, participating in activities such as classroom discussions and field trips broadens their cross-cultural communication skills and deepens their intercultural understanding.

教学方式

The course is organised in four main segments. The first segment, entitled ‘Foundations of Urbanization’, introduces the course’s key themes and builds up theoretical foundations about urbanisation and economic development. Segment two, entitled ‘process of urban development’, focuses on the physical dimension of the cities, exploring the power of urban landscapes and how the cities are built. The third segment, entitled ‘People and Place’, highlights the social dimension of urbanisation and urban socio-spatial inequality. Finally, the fourth segment, entitled ‘Governing global urbanisation’, explores changing urban governance and policy-making in response to urban problems. Meanwhile, to provide students with guidance on key aspects of comparative urban studies, the course also includes two seminars on research methods, ‘Comparative Urban Research Methods’ and ‘Urban Research Practices in China’. Besides, two field trips to Lujiazui, Pudong (A case of building a global city) and Songjiang (a case of suburban development) would be arranged.

教学内容与进度安排

Schedule (tentative)

Part I Foundations of Urbanisation

Week 1 Introduction: urbanisation, the urban future, the way to look at your cities

This lecture sets the scene for the course, introducing the objectives, approaches and concepts of urban studies. In particular, it offers students both conceptual and empirical orientation for understanding the ongoing urbanization in China.

Readings

  • Logan and Fainstein S.S. (2008), Chapter 1, Urban China in Comparative Perspective in Urban China in Transition, Oxford: Blackwell Publishing Ltd, 1-23
  • Philips E.B (2009). Chapter 2, Thinking about Cities in City Lights: urban-suburban life in the Global Society, New York: Oxford University Press, 36-65

Reflection Questions

  • How does the theory of urbanisation resemble and differ from your previous understanding of it?
  • Why does urbanisation in China have significant implications for global urban studies？

 

Week 2 The origins and growth of Cities in the world: urbanisation and the urban system

This lecture provides the global context of urbanization and urban development. It includes three parts: 1) the origins of the cities, 2) contemporary urbanization, and 3) theories of urbanization and economic development.

Readings

  • Brenner, N., and C. Schmid.  (2014). The ‘Urban Age’ in Question.International Journal of Urban and Regional Research 38 (3): 731–755.
  • United Nations, Department of Economic and Social Affairs, Population Division (2019). World Urbanization Prospects: The 2018 Revision, Highlights (online)

Reflection Questions

  • Based on the history of urbanisation in the world, what do you think are the most important drivers of urbanisation?

 

Week 3 Economic reform and urbanisation in China

China has witnessed rapid urbanization since the launch of economic reform. However, urbanization remains relatively behind industrialization. The lecture in the third week focuses on the pattern of urbanization in China and explores the underlying forces of this pattern. It is argued that urban-rural relations are central to understanding the process.  The lecture includes three parts: 1) Urban-rural relations in China; 2) Urbanization in the pre-reform era; 3) Urbanization in the post-reform era.

Readings

  • Zhu J., Zhu J., Zhu M. and Xiao Y. (2019). Urbanization for rural development: Spatial paradigm shifts toward inclusive urban-rural integrated development in China. Journal of Rural Studies, 71, 94-103
  • Friedmann, John (2005). Introduction: Becoming Urban in China; Chapter 1 Historical Traces. in China’s Urban Transition, University of Minnesota Press: Minneapolis: xiii-xxv; 1-17

Reflection Questions

  • What's new about China's latest urbanization?

 

Week 4 Globalization and World Cities

International integration arises from the interchange of world views, products, ideas and other aspects of culture. Then what are the ways globalization works itself out in (and through) cities? This lecture deals with this question by discussing 1) the world urban system under globalization; 2) the hypothesis and paradigm on global cities; 3) New trends of global city development; and 4) the case study of Shanghai.

Readings

  • Hill R.C. and Kim J.W. (2000). Global Cities and Developmental States: New York, Tokyo and Seoul. Urban Studies, 37(12), 2167– 2195
  • Brenner, Neil and Keil, Roger (Eds.) (2006). Editors’ Introduction & Introduction to Part I. in The Global Cities Reader. London and New York: Routledge., 1-22

Reflection Questions

  • How do you think the recent trend of de-globalisation might influence cities?

 

Week 5 Field trip to Lujiazui, Pudong

Lujiazui reflects 1) the effort of Shanghai to use urban space as a basis for economic growth; and 2) the urban impacts of globalization processes and the dynamics of the global-local nexus. An urban planner is invited on-site to talk about the history of the development of Lujiazui and the challenges it faces. The trip is to visit the Lujiazui corridor, Binjiang Avenue, Lujiazui Central Green Park, as well as high-rise residential development in the area.

Readings

  • Xue C. Q. L., Zhai H. and Mitchenere B. (2011) Shaping Lujiazui: The Formation and Building of the CBD in Pudong, Shanghai, Journal of Urban Design, 16:02, 209-232
  • Olds, K. (1997) Globalizing Shanghai: the ‘global intelligence corps’ and the building of Pudong, Cities, 14, pp. 109–123.

Reflection Questions

  • Do you think that global city theory can be applied to understand Shanghai's development? Why do you think so?

 

Part II Process of Urban Change

Week 6 The City as Text: Architecture and Urban Design

This lecture aims to show how architecture and urban design are linked to the dynamics of urban social, cultural, and economic change. First, it establishes the importance of architecture and design for people in more general terms. Second, it reviews successive design phases and explains the interdependencies between society and design. Finally, it discusses the restless urban landscape in China.

Readings

  • Shen J. And Wu Fu. (2012). Restless Urban Landscapes in China: A Case Study of Three Projects in Shanghai. Journal of Urban Affairs, 34(3), 255-277
  • Knox, P. L. (1991). The restless urban landscape: Economic and sociocultural change and the transformation of metropolitan Washington, DC. Annals of the Association of American Geographers, 81(2), 181–209

Reflection Questions

  • From the perspectives discussed in this lecture, can you explain how innovation complexes have recently emerged as important urban elements around the world?

 

Week 7 Suburbanization and suburban development

Suburbanization is defined as the combination of noncentral population and economic growth with urban spatial expansion. Despite normative preferences in official planning and city-building discourse for a dense and centralized urban form, suburbanization remains the dominant mode in which cities are built worldwide. The lecture will first offer concepts of suburbanisation from a comparative perspective. It will then focus on suburban growth in China. Afterwards, the dynamics of recent suburban new town development are examined in detail.

Readings

  • Fishman, R. (1987). Chapter 1 Bourgeois Utopias: Visions of Suburbia in Bourgeois Utopias: The Rise and Fall of Suburbia. New York: Basic Books, 3-17
  • Zhou, Y. and Ma, L. J. C. (2000). Economic restructuring and suburbanization in China. Urban Geography, 21(3), 205-236.
  • Ekers M, Hamel P and Keil R (2012) Governing suburbia: Modalities and mechanisms of suburban governance. Regional Studies 46(3):405–422

Reflection Questions

  • Do different suburbanisms have common characteristics and dynamics?

 

Week 8 Field trip to Songjiang New Town

Songjiang New Town represents a role model of new town development in Shanghai. Its development process reflects the effort to remake the suburbs as an ideal living location. The trip will include flagship projects in Songjiang New Town, i.e. metro line no.9, Songjiang University Town, Thames Town and Central Green Belt.

Readings

  • Shen J. and Wu F. (2017). The Suburb as a Space of Capital Accumulation: The Development of New Towns in Shanghai, China. Antipode, 49(3): 761–780

Reflection Questions

  • Do you think that there are 'suburbs (jiaoqu)' in Chinese cities?

 

Part III People and Places

Week 9 Housing and Neighborhood Change

This lecture examines the dynamics of neighbourhood change, focusing on how people operate in housing markets and how their residential mobility plays a role in shaping and reshaping neighbourhoods. The lecture includes five parts: 1) fundamental dynamics of neighbourhood change; 2) housing market and neighbourhood change; 3) Residential mobility and neighbourhood change; 4) housing market gatekeepers; 5) the example of gentrification.

Readings

  • Knox and McCarthy (2012). Chapter 9, How neighbourhoods change in Urbanization: an Introduction to Urban Geography, Boston: Pearson, 207-232
  • Wei F. and Knox P. L. (2014) Neighborhood Change in Metropolitan America, 1990 to 2010. Urban Affairs Review, 50(4), 459–489
  • He S. (2012). Two waves of gentrification and emerging rights issues in Guangzhou, China. Environment and Planning A, volume 44, pages 2817 – 2833

Reflection Questions

  • As neighbourhoods evolve, how can urban policies help shape and sustain social cohesion and community identity?

 

Week 10-11 Migration and the City

The essence of city life is “the being together of strangers”. New arrivals play a significant role in shaping urban development through their interaction with one another and with existing groups—striving for economic and social success while dealing with their own cultural identity. This lecture draws attention to the way migrants live in the city. It includes four parts: 1) Immigration and cities; 2) urban experiences of minority ethnic groups; 3) internal migration in China; 4) urban villages: urban experience of migrants.

Readings

  • Liu Y., Li Z., Liu Y. and Chen H. (2014). Growth of rural migrant enclaves in Guangzhou, China: Agency, everyday practice and social mobility. Urban Studies, published online first.
  • Wu F., Zhang F. and Webster C. (2013) Informality and the Development and Demolition of Urban Villages in the Chinese Peri-urban Area. Urban Studies, 50(10), 1919-1934

Reflection Questions

  • How can urban policies better balance the instrumental use of diversity for economic gain with the need to ensure equitable access to resources and rights for all city dwellers, including migrants?

 

Part IV Governing Urbanisation

Week 12 Sustainability and the development of eco-cities

There has always been awareness and anxiety about rapid urbanisation and continuing or worsening environmental despoliation. This lecture focuses on green urbanism and the recent fever of eco-cities. First, it introduces the origin and evolution of green urbanism and eco-cities. Second, it discusses analytical perspectives on the dynamics of eco-cities. The final part then identifies the characteristics and underlying driving forces of development in China.

Readings

  • Caprotti F, Springer C and Harmer N (2015) ‘Eco’ for whom? Envisioning eco-urbanism in the Sino-Singapore Tianjin Eco-city, China. International Journal of Urban and Regional Research 39(3): 495–517
  • Pow C.P. and Neo H. (2013). Seeing Red over Green: Contesting Urban Sustainabilities in China. Urban Studies, 50(11) 2256–2274

Reflection Questions

  • How can green urbanism be integrated with broader strategies for promoting sustainable livelihoods and fostering more resilient communities?
